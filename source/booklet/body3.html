Chapter five · March – November 1942

Ceylon, home, and a new army

With Japan now in the war, the 6th and 7th Divisions were recalled from the Middle East. Part of the 6th Division, including Cyril's unit, was landed in Ceylon to help defend the island against a feared Japanese attack.

What his unit was doing

The unit embarked at Suez in the transports Westernland, Clan McTavish, Gogra and Cromarty, sailed on 13 March 1942, called at Aden and reached Colombo on 27 March. The first big Japanese air raid on Colombo came soon after they arrived. The unit camp, at "Maddagedera" as Moran spells it, became an engineering centre for the force. Moran remembered "hard work, intense heat; followed by torrential rain".

Moran p. 47.

From his own record

27 Mar 1942: disembarks in Ceylon. 7 Aug 1942: disembarks at Melbourne "ex HMT Ekma". The handwritten date could be read as 4 August, but the typed discharge summary and the unit diary both give 7 August.

13 Aug–6 Sep: leave. 15 Sep 1942: transferred to 6 Aust Div Ordnance Workshop. 3 Nov: entrains for Queensland.

NAA: B883, SX8216, pp. 5, 6, 18; AWM52 14/2/2/9 ("the period 13.7.42 to 7.8.42 having been spent at sea").

What his unit was doing

After arriving at Melbourne, the unit camped at Mangalore, Victoria, marched through Melbourne with the 17th Brigade, and in September moved to Minimbah and Greta in the Hunter Valley, NSW. There it was broken up "as an Army Ordnance Workshop, and a Divisional Ordnance Workshop of H.Q. and two Bde Group Workshops". On 15 September, the day Cyril's transfer took effect, "No I Group 6 Div Wkshop moved from Minimbah to Greta".

AWM52 14/2/2/9, Aug–Sep 1942.

"No. 1 Group" and "No. 2 Group"

The 6 Div Ordnance Workshop was organised as a headquarters and two brigade group workshops. Each was a self-contained repair section meant to move with one of the division's infantry brigades. In the war diary these appear as "No. 1 Group" and "No. 2 Group". By mid-1943 Cyril's record calls his section the "16 Bde Sec 6 Aust Div W/S", the section supporting the 16th Infantry Brigade. These sections later became separately numbered brigade workshops. Moran mentions the 2/119 and 2/120 Brigade Workshops, and Cyril joined the 2/121st in July 1943.

Not to be confused with his trade grading, "Group I Blacksmith". That was a pay classification for skilled tradesmen (see "What the trade terms mean").

AWM52 14/2/2/9; NAA p. 6; Moran p. 47.

moderateThe evidence suggests: he was in No. 1 Group at Greta

His transfer date (15 September 1942) is the same day the diary records No. 1 Group of the 6 Div Workshop moving from Minimbah to Greta, and by 1943 he was in the 16 Bde Section. It is likely that "No. 1 Group" was the 16 Brigade group, but the diary seen so far does not say this in so many words.

Based on: NAA p. 6 (transfer 15 Sep 1942; "16 Bde Sec", 12 Jul 1943); AWM52 14/2/2/9 (15 Sep 1942).
From his own record

8 Dec 1942: embarks at Townsville on the Duntroon. 12 Dec 1942: disembarks in New Guinea.

NAA: B883, SX8216, pp. 6, 18.

Chapter six · December 1942 – February 1944

New Guinea

Cyril reached New Guinea in December 1942, as the fighting at Buna, Gona and Sanananda was coming to an end. He stayed for fourteen months. From December 1942 the army's ordnance workshops were transferred to the new Australian Electrical and Mechanical Engineers (AEME), and their privates became craftsmen. In September 1943 his mother addressed her letter to "Cfn C.R. Phillips, 2/121 Aust Bde Workshops A.E.M.E."

From his own record

12 Dec 1942: disembarks in New Guinea with the 16 Bde Section, 6 Aust Div Workshop.

12 Jul 1943: taken on strength by 2/121 Aust Bde Workshops "from 16 Bde Sec 6 Aust Div W/S".

5–23 Sep 1943: to 128 Australian General Hospital (NYD, "not yet diagnosed"), then to the New Guinea Details Depot, then back to 2/121 Bde Workshops on 23 September.

29 Jan 1944: to 2/1 Australian General Hospital (then at Bootless Bay, about 20 km south-east of Port Moresby) for investigation. 1 Feb 1944: re-graded from Group I Blacksmith to Group I Fitter (the specialty is written "Ins").

13 Feb 1944: carried aboard the 2/1st Australian Hospital Ship Manunda at Port Moresby.

NAA: B883, SX8216, pp. 2, 6, 10.

high · about 85%The evidence suggests: the Port Moresby area

Everything that can be placed points to Port Moresby. He sailed from Townsville on the Duntroon, the usual run to Moresby. Both hospitals he was admitted to were in the Port Moresby area: 128 AGH at Port Moresby, and 2/1 AGH at Bootless Bay. In July–August 1943 the 2/121 Brigade Ordnance Field Park at Port Moresby lists "2/121 Aust Bde W/shops" among the units it serviced. He was evacuated from Port Moresby in 1944.

Based on: NAA pp. 6, 18 (Townsville–Duntroon–NG; 128 AGH; 2/1 AGH; Manunda at Port Moresby). AWM52 13/40/13/1 (2/121 Bde OFP, Jul–Aug 1943). AWM 075198 caption ("128th General Hospital, Port Moresby"). 2/1 AGH at Bootless Bay, Sep 1943–Jun 1944 (Our Contribution wiki; VWMA). Caveat: the war diaries of 16 Bde Section and 2/121 Bde Workshops were not found online, so detachments elsewhere (for example Wau or Milne Bay) cannot be ruled out.
moderateThe evidence suggests: what the 2/121st supported

The 2/121 Brigade Ordnance Field Park had previously been the "17 Bde Section, 6 Div OFP". That suggests the 2/121st Brigade units, Cyril's workshop included, were tied to the 6th Division's 17th Brigade, which fought at Wau and Salamaua in 1943.

Based on: AWM52 13/40/13/1 (title and opening entries). Not confirmed for the workshop itself.

Chapter seven · 1944 and after

Home, discharge and later life

From his own record

17–22 Feb 1944: lands at Brisbane, travels on No. 2 Australian Ambulance Train to 115 AGH (Heidelberg, Victoria), where essential hypertension is diagnosed (19 Feb), and then goes to 121 AGH (22 Feb), which had moved to Northfield, South Australia.

8–10 Mar: medical board, Class B. Discharged to the SA Lines of Communication Area Details Depot.

25 Mar 1944: marries Irene May. She becomes his next of kin.

26 Apr–26 May: attached to SA L of C Area Workshops AEME. 6–21 Jun: to Queensland, posted to 7 Technical Maintenance Section, attached Advanced LHQ Signals. 24 Jun–3 Jul: Linemen Mechanics Course No. 70 at 1 ATTD.

21 Aug: medical class Temporary D. 25 Sep: back in Adelaide "for discharge". 2–18 Oct: 105 Australian Military Hospital (hypertension, mild). Final board Class D on 17 October.

28 Nov 1944: discharged at Wayville, medically unfit. Total service 1,607 days: 1,129 overseas (691 in the Middle East and Ceylon, 437 in New Guinea).

NAA: B883, SX8216, pp. 1, 5–8, 14, 17, 18, 21.

His last papers call him "Sig Phillips", a reflection of his final posting with a signals headquarters. The Office of Australian War Graves recorded him in 1977 as a Craftsman, Corps of Electrical and Mechanical Engineers.

"This is a story of engineers and tradesmen… It tells of Australian civilians who were trained as soldiers and then applied their technical skills in war."Brigadier George Moran, Preface to The Story of the 2/2 Army Field Workshops (1964)

His medals

From his own record

His medal card is ticked for six awards: the 1939–45 Star, Africa Star, Pacific Star, Defence Medal, War Medal 1939–45 and Australia Service Medal 1939–45. They were applied for in March 1954 and posted on 8 April 1954 (as read) to his Borrika address.

NAA: B883, SX8216, p. 9.

for the familyWorth knowing

The six ticks match what his service would be expected to earn: the 1939–45 Star and Africa Star for the Middle East, the Pacific Star for New Guinea, and the Defence Medal, War Medal and Australia Service Medal for his service overall. If, as seems probable, he served in the Tobruk siege, he would also have qualified as a "Rat of Tobruk". That was an association, not a medal. A next of kin can ask Defence Honours and Awards to confirm his entitlement. Replica sets can be bought privately for family display.

Based on: NAA p. 9 (medal card). The Tobruk inference in Chapter three.

Later life

From the records

Cyril died on 7 January 1977, aged 59. He was buried at Dudley Park on 11 January 1977. His widow, Mrs I.M. Phillips, was then living at Mile End, Adelaide. The Repatriation Department's file reference for him was MX 5497.

NAA: B883, SX8216, pp. 19–20 (DVA notice, 20 Jan 1977; Office of Australian War Graves card).
