Chapter two · October 1940 – March 1941

Egypt and Libya: the first desert campaign

From Helwan, near Cairo, the 2/2nd Army Field Workshops moved into the Western Desert in December 1940 as corps troops, attached to the British XIII Corps, just as General O'Connor's offensive drove the Italian army out of Egypt. The unit's job was to keep the army moving. It recovered broken-down and captured vehicles, repaired trucks, guns and watches, and above all kept the few British "I" tanks fit to fight.

What his unit was doing

5 Dec 1940: Main Shop and No. 6 Recovery Section to Amiriya. 16 Dec: to Fuka. 23–27 Dec: 6 Recovery Section forms the advance party at "Kilo 104" on the coast near Sidi Barrani.

3–5 Jan 1941: Bardia falls. Main Shop and "all 6 Rec Sec" work at the 7th Royal Tank Regiment's lager. Captains Naismith and Redpath and their teams get 20 tanks fit for the next battle.

21–22 Jan: the capture of Tobruk. A detachment of 6 Recovery Section "went 'in' with the tanks, put five disabled ones into service again when under fire and won three military medals" (Sgt Allison, Ptes Smith and Dunning). 24 Jan: Main Shop to Ain el Gazala.

Feb 1941: 6 Recovery Section pushes on via Giovanni Berta and Tecnis. The Main Shop works at Maddalena near Barce. 9–29 Mar: 6 Recovery Section works at Mersa Matruh (432 jobs) and returns to Amiriya.

AWM52 14/2/2/2 to 14/2/2/5; Moran pp. 13–22, 36.

moderateThe evidence suggests: Christmas 1940 at Fuka

If Cyril was in 6 Recovery Section, he most likely spent Christmas 1940 at Fuka. Moran says the Main Shop worked there until 26 December and moved to Kilo 104 "accompanied by the flexible 6 Rec. Sec." the next day. The unit's seven-course Christmas dinner at Fuka, with "a free bottle of Abbotts lager beer each", was photographed. The diary has a 6 Recovery Section advance party at Kilo 104 from 23 December, so he may instead have been with that party.

Based on: Moran p. 13; AWM52 14/2/2/2 (16 and 23 Dec 1940); AWM photos P01522.001 and P01522.003.
From his own record

His record has no entries between his arrival in October 1940 and June 1941. That means no wounds, illness, transfers or offences, so he stayed with his unit throughout this campaign. His later medals include the Africa Star, which was awarded for service in North Africa.

NAA: B883, SX8216, pp. 5, 9.

Chapter three · April – July 1941

Tobruk: the siege and "the windmill"

In March 1941 most of the 2/2nd Army Field Workshops went back to Egypt to prepare for the expedition to Greece. No. 5 Recovery Section went to Greece. On 5 April No. 6 Recovery Section left Amiriya for Tobruk, and it arrived before Rommel's Afrika Korps surrounded the fortress. For three months it was the only part of the unit inside the siege, keeping the defenders' vehicles and guns in action.

What his unit was doing

8 Apr 1941: 6 Recovery Section, under Captain J.W. Redpath, in Tobruk. 11 Apr: "No. 6 Recovery Section moved out to position on Derna Road about 1 mile West of Tobruk." 22 Apr: Redpath writes from Tobruk that the section has had no losses.

Moran: "The other absent member, '6 Recovery Section', was besieged in Tobruk. Their site was at the windmill and with craftsmen of the 2/1 Army Field Workshops and the LADs helped in the successful defence of the fortress."

7–11 Jul 1941: 6 Recovery Section leaves Tobruk. "Capt. Redpath and No. 6 Rec. Sec. marched in from Tobruk" to Barbara, Palestine, on 11 July.

AWM52 14/2/1/4 (2/1 AFW, 8 and 11 Apr 1941); AWM52 14/2/2/5, 14/2/2/6; Moran pp. 36, 41.

From his own record

14 June 1941: admitted to "4 Aust Gen Hosp" with influenza. 17 June: discharged (enteritis) and rejoined his unit the same day.

NAA: B883, SX8216, p. 5.

probable · about 70–75%The evidence suggests: he was in Tobruk during the siege

The 2/4th Australian General Hospital was inside besieged Tobruk from 26 March to 21 October 1941. In June 1941 Cyril was treated at "4 AGH" and was back with his unit on the day he was discharged. That is only possible if his unit was close by, and the only part of 2/2 AFW in Tobruk was No. 6 Recovery Section. He had not gone to Greece with No. 5 Section, because his record has no Greek service, and he appears on 6 Recovery Section guard rosters later in 1941.

Based on: NAA p. 5 (14–17 Jun 1941). 2/4 AGH location: Our Contribution wiki. Moran pp. 36, 41. AWM52 14/2/2/7–8 (guard rosters, Chapter four). Caveat: his record never names his section, and twelve 6 Recovery Section men on leave could not get back into Tobruk in April 1941 (AWM52 14/2/2/5, 15 Apr). A 2/4 AGH admission register would settle it.
moderateThe evidence suggests: where "the windmill" was

Moran does not say where the windmill stood. The only fix on the section's position is the war diary's "Derna Road about 1 mile West of Tobruk" (11 April 1941). The windmill was most likely a wind-pump or well-head on or near the Derna Road, just west of the town and well inside the perimeter. No wartime map marking it has yet been found.

Based on: AWM52 14/2/1/4, 11 Apr 1941; Moran p. 41. To check: AWM Tobruk maps (LIB52519; the Italian 1:50,000 "Tobruch" sheet) and the 2/1 AFW diaries for May–June 1941.

Chapter four · July 1941 – March 1942

Palestine, Transjordan and Lebanon

After the Syrian campaign the reunited 2/2nd Army Field Workshops served the Allied armies in Palestine, Transjordan and Lebanon. In this period Cyril's name finally appears in his unit's own papers. The daily Routine Orders list him on the camp guard several times.

What his unit was doing

11 Jul 1941: 6 Recovery Section reaches Barbara, near Ascalon (Ashkelon), and goes on seven days' leave, then to an infantry training school. Aug 1941: the unit moves to Beit Jirja, then Khassa. 27 Aug–12 Sep: 6 Recovery Section services the 5th Indian Division at pumping station H4, on the oil pipeline in Transjordan.

Oct 1941: the unit moves to the Ablah barracks in Lebanon, 3,000 feet up between the Lebanon and Anti-Lebanon ranges. 11–13 Nov: 6 Recovery Section goes to Mafraq and H4 on road patrol for the Ninth Army. Winter brings snow, and more than 100 frozen and cracked engine blocks are welded. 27 Jan 1942: the unit leaves Syria and reaches Port Said on 20 February.

AWM52 14/2/2/6–8; Moran pp. 42–46.

Named in unit orders

22 Sep 1941, Khassa: Routine Orders M/S No. 98. Duty section for 23 September: "6 Recovery Sec." The guard includes "SX8216 Phillips, C.R.", alongside 6 Recovery Section men such as K. Smith, MM.

29 Sep 1941, Khassa: Routine Orders No. 4. Duty section: "H.Q. & 6 Rec Secs". The guard includes Phillips, C.R. and Dunning, MM, of 6 Recovery Section.

29 Oct 1941, Ablah: Routine Orders No. 25, signed by Lt-Col Moran. Phillips C.R. is on the guard for 30 October (duty section: MT Section).

6 Nov 1941, Ablah: Routine Orders No. 32. Duty section: "H.Q. and 6 Aust Rec Sec". The guard includes SX8216 Phillips C.R.

AWM52 14/2/2/7 (Aug–Sep 1941) and 14/2/2/8 (Oct–Nov 1941).

strong · about 80%The evidence suggests: No. 6 Recovery Section

Three of the four guards that name him were drawn from 6 Recovery Section, and on two of them he stands beside known members of that section, including two of its Military Medal winners. Together with his South Australian enlistment, his voyage and the Tobruk hospital entry, this makes 6 Recovery Section by far the most likely place for Cyril.

Based on: the four Routine Orders above. NAA pp. 5, 18. Moran pp. 9, 12, 41. Against: one guard (29 Oct) came from MT Section. When the unit was reorganised in September 1942, 5 and 6 Recovery Sections were kept "as complete as possible" in No. 2 Army Ordnance Workshop, but Cyril was posted to 6 Div Ordnance Workshop (AWM52 14/2/2/9).
From his own record

12 Mar 1942: embarks in the Middle East. 27 Mar 1942: disembarks in Ceylon.

NAA: B883, SX8216, p. 5.
